About the names, pins and boundary lines

Names. Ancient labels follow the biblical index. Present-day labels follow the source’s modern site names; a ruin can retain its ancient name. Both names remain visible when you select a place.

Pins. A pin marks a source-listed site or a representative point for a larger area. Open the location selector to compare other recorded proposals. Entries without coordinates stay in the searchable collection.

Time periods. Choose Ancient Canaan (1400 BCE), First Temple (850 BCE), Second Temple (30 CE), Byzantine (550 CE), Ottoman (1600 CE), pre-WWI (1913 CE), or Modern. Each is a dated snapshot within a broader period. The same place and reference pins remain available when the period changes.

Historical territories. Cliopatria, from the Seshat Global History Databank, supplies regional reconstructions of political territories. These show broad geographic context; they do not supply every city-state, provincial or local administrative boundary. Canaan is a period and regional heading, not a single kingdom outline. The source does not encode competing historical border reconstructions. Source labels and date intervals are preserved in the downloadable data.

Compare. Keep the same map position while switching periods, adjust territory shading, or overlay present-day lines. Ancient/biblical and present-day place names switch independently. The background uses modern land and water outlines.

Present-day boundaries. Natural Earth supplies the modern boundary layer, with disputed lines dashed. The background uses modern land and water outlines.
